EMERGENTOLOGY · A quantitative science of recursive structure formation

The Wright Emergence Functional

A reconstruction of the historical “Wright Force” as a measurable state-space quantity rather than a claimed fifth fundamental interaction.

The core quantity

WE(t) = αC Ċ + αA(−d/dt log d(x,A)) − αS Ṡprod + αRR

The functional combines coherence growth, contraction toward an attractor, entropy-production cost, and—when independently defined—recursive feedback strength.

Scientific boundary: this is an effective emergence metric. It is not evidence for a new fundamental force, exchange particle, cognitive field, or violation of thermodynamics.

Emergence threshold

W₀ = inf { w : P(persistent structure | WE ≥ w) ≥ p* }

The empirical problem is to determine whether a stable threshold exists and whether it predicts emergent organization better than simpler metrics.

Recursive attractors

xn+1 = F(xn),    d(Fⁿ(x₀),A) → 0

Emergentology studies contraction rate, basin geometry, persistence, perturbation robustness, and cross-scale transfer.

What Lean proves

The formalization proves elementary properties of fixed points, forward invariance, the algebraic Wright functional, additive coherence contributions, entropy penalties, and monotonic threshold logic.

The real research target: a normalized quantity that predicts structure formation across unrelated dynamical systems with fewer fitted parameters than standard baselines.

Where to test first

Coupled oscillators, recurrent neural networks, cellular automata, reaction-diffusion systems, multi-agent coordination, and control systems provide clean benchmark environments.

Conclusion

Emergentology becomes scientifically interesting when it stops asking whether emergence is a mysterious new force and starts asking whether structure formation has reusable invariants.
